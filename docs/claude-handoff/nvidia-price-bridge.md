# Why Nvidia fell from $230 to $125 even as its earnings outlook rose

November 21, 2026. Prices are regular-session closes. Nvidia fiscal 2028 ends in January 2028.

**At $125.53 I would describe Nvidia as cheap on near-term earnings and close to fair value on a conservative cash-flow model.** The stock fell 45.5% from $230.48 on October 8. Nvidia subsequently raised its fiscal 2028 revenue growth outlook from about 70% to 75% and kept the 72–73% gross-margin range. Meanwhile, the CEO changed, scrutiny increased and the latest disclosures showed more cash tied up in the buildout. The price move therefore reflects a lower valuation on the earnings in our model. The precise cause of each part of the selloff is uncertain. ## What changed and how it affects value

1. **Reported demand improved.** Q3 sales of $115.84B beat Nvidia's August $108B midpoint by $7.84B. Q3 GAAP gross margin was 74.8% against August guidance of 74% ±0.5 percentage points. The August call already anticipated a Q4 gross-margin trough of 71–72% and a FY2028 recovery to 72–73%. Memory costs were a known risk at $230. [August call](https://investor.nvidia.com/files/content_files/TRANSCRIPT_-NVIDIA-Corp-NVDA-US-Q2-2027-Earnings-Call-26-August-2026-5_00-PM-ET.pdf) · [November earnings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/q3fy27pr.htm)
2. **Funding and collection exposure became more visible.** Supplier commitments rose from $279B to $390B, mainly for memory. That can secure supply in an upcycle and tie up cash if demand disappoints. Collection time rose from 60 to 64 days and inventory from $31.6B to $39.7B. Management attributed this to Rubin shipment timing, longer terms with certain investment-grade customers, and inventory for the launch. The $105B phased Ohio guarantee was already disclosed in August. November added $9.5B in power and systems guarantees for total maximum gross exposure of $118B. Reported contingent neocloud agreements fell from $36B to $32B. Maximum guarantees are contingent and the company reported no payments through Q3. [Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm) · [Q3 CFO commentary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/q3fy27cfocommentary.htm)
3. **Leadership and regulatory risk changed.** Jensen Huang resigned on November 12 and CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO. California issued an investigative subpoena on November 19. Reuters reported November 20 that the SEC inquiry included potential accounting issues. These developments create uncertainty around oversight and the reliability of future forecasts. The outcomes were unresolved at the evidence cutoff. The November 19 BIS rule also narrowed the circumstances under which covered parties can use certain advanced compute remotely. Nvidia's Q4 guide already excludes China Data Center compute sales. The effect on reselling cloud capacity elsewhere remains uncertain. [Nvidia announcement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition) · [California subpoena](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-serves-investigative-subpoena-nvidia) · [Reuters report](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/exclusiveus-secs-nvidia-probe-is-examining-potential-accounting-issues-sources-say-5042460) · [BIS rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23529/addressing-remote-access-to-advanced-semiconductors)
4. **The broader market was also selling AI exposure.** Nvidia was already down 32.5% from October 8 by the November 17 close, before that afternoon's earnings report. It fell another 19.3% from that close to November 20. On November 20 alone Nvidia lost 14.0%, the PHLX Semiconductor Index fell 10.7%, and the S&P 500 fell 3.7%. The joint move supports a sector-wide repricing alongside Nvidia-specific risks. It supplies no firm allocation of the loss among individual events. Custom accelerators were a pre-existing risk to Nvidia's share of future workloads and pricing. They belong in the long-run cash model. The October–November public disclosures do not quantify a newly lost volume of Nvidia orders that explains the share-price move.

## Why hold at $125 and what would make it a buy

At $125.53, Nvidia trades at 7.52× the guidance-based EPS model and 9.47× the report's more conservative $13.25 normal-case EPS. In the three November 2027 scenarios, $73.58 bearish represents 41% downside, $185.51 normal represents 48% upside, and $330.28 best represents 163% upside, excluding dividends. The close is only about 6% above the separate $118.19 cash valuation. Calling it expensive without naming the model would overstate the case.

The current DCF discounts five annual cash flows of approximately $217B, $242B, $227B, $213B and $197B. At 3.5% perpetual growth and a 10% discount rate, the FCF model yields $118 per share. At 9% it yields $138 and at 8% it yields $167. Holding all of today's cash-flow assumptions fixed, $230 would require a discount rate of about 6.7% at the same perpetual growth rate. This is a sensitivity to illustrate how demanding the earlier price was under today's assumptions. It is not an estimate of the market's October discount rate. A 20% discount to $118 gives a $95 entry while that cash outlook holds. If disclosures support better cash conversion or more durable margins, a higher entry can be justified. The report explains the operating margins, reinvestment and partner funding behind these cash flows.
