# Nvidia demand, supply constraints, and valuation — November 2026

Prepared November 21, 2026 · Financial evidence through October 25, 2026; subsequent policy and legal developments through November 20. Price reference: November 20 regular-session close. USD unless stated.

**Hold or watch at $125.53. A purchase near $95 would provide a 20% discount to the $118 cash-flow estimate.** A normal earnings recovery supports a November 2027 target of $186, but the bearish case reaches $74. The distinction matters. A buyer at today’s price can make money if investors continue to capitalize fiscal 2028 earnings. A long-term owner also needs those earnings to turn into cash after the customer base has been financed. The closing price fell 14.0% on November 20, 22.9% from November 13 and 38.7% from October 30. The most useful starting point is shipments and revenue, followed by customer deployment plans. Nvidia’s total quarterly revenue was $115.8 billion; Data Center accounted for $107.6 billion. Revenue attributed to direct customers headquartered in the United States was $72.9 billion, or about 63% of the total. Customer headquarters do not establish installation location, and purchases through foreign manufacturers can serve American end customers. (1. Customers’ investment budgets also support ongoing infrastructure demand. ## 2. Supply remains binding, but it is more than a wafer shortage

The evidence is strongest when Nvidia’s position is checked against its suppliers. Micron says most of its calendar 2027 HBM bit supply is already covered by agreements, with substantial price increases, and expects tighter memory conditions in 2027–2028. TSMC describes a sizable demand/supply gap and is investing $60–64B in 2026. These are supplier-wide statements, not a promise of a specific number of Nvidia GPUs. (3. See [3] Manufacturing and component constraints below.)

| Constraint | Evidence or dependency | Effect on the forecast |
|---|---|---|
| Leading-edge wafers | TSMC demand exceeds available supply | More orders do not immediately create more finished accelerators |
| HBM and substrates | Nvidia is committing more to inputs; Micron has contracted most 2027 HBM supply | Can limit shipments and raise unit cost |
| Packaging and system integration | Nvidia is expanding its supplier network | Secured components still must become qualified, working systems |
| Powered data-center space | A customer must have a usable site | Available hardware can coexist with delayed deployments |
| Customer capital and offtake | Allocation considers financing and readiness | An announced project may not qualify for near-term allocation |

(View source. See [3] Manufacturing and component constraints below.) · (Allocation criteria. See [2] Demand, allocation, and management guidance below.)

Nvidia’s $390B supply/capacity commitments, up from $279B, are a strong statement of production intent. About $203B falls in FY2028 and $92B in FY2029. They also increase exposure if demand disappoints; they cannot be converted into shipment revenue without product costs, mix and timing. (3. See [3] Manufacturing and component constraints below.)

**Assessment:** the public evidence favors a manufacturing and deployment constraint over a present shortage of customer interest. However, supplier shortages do not prove that every Nvidia product remains oversubscribed after every regulatory shock. No reviewed disclosure provides a SKU-level production ceiling, binding customer orders and cancellation terms covering the same future periods.

**The November 20 statement came after Nvidia’s earnings call.** It therefore cannot be assumed to have been incorporated into November 17 guidance. Nor should the speech be modeled as a fully implemented ban with a known financial cost. It could make relocation a less dependable response to domestic permitting delays.

The operating test is straightforward: if demand lost to restrictions can be replaced by another lawful buyer with financing and a ready site, total shipments can remain supply limited. If not, the same policy shock reduces sales. Management’s assessment supports the first outcome near term, but public disclosures do not quantify the available replacement demand. A shortage can absorb some demand loss; its existence alone does not specify how much.

## 4. Custom chips threaten Nvidia’s incremental share and economics

Google and Amazon can expand their own accelerators while also buying more Nvidia GPUs. Alphabet expects a meaningful TPU system-sales ramp in 2027; AWS’s Nvidia deployment plan demonstrates simultaneous demand. The investment consequence is less certainty that each incremental dollar of AI spending flows to Nvidia. (5. See [5] American customer demand and deployment plans below.) (8. See [8] Custom chips and market-share comparability below.)

The updated J.P. Morgan comparison puts Nvidia at 28% of selected announced frontier-lab capacity, measured in GW; the earlier 39% was revised. The historical 80–90% refers to estimated AI-chip revenue share in a different population and period. Neither the capacity figure nor the earlier three-vendor revenue proxy establishes current pure-chip revenue share. This report does not convert one into the other. (8. See [8] Custom chips and market-share comparability below.)

**Current reported revenue comparison.** Nvidia’s $60.1B hyperscale sales were **55.9% of its own $107.6B Data Center revenue** and **51.9% of its $115.844B company revenue**. A limited comparison across three suppliers is **81.0%**, calculated as Nvidia Data Center $107.6B ÷ ($107.6B + AMD Data Center $8.546B + Broadcom AI semiconductor $16.7B). The fiscal quarters ended October 25, September 26 and August 2, respectively. The buckets include some CPUs, networking and system sales, and exclude other suppliers and much internally used silicon. The 81.0% calculation measures relative reported revenue for these three selected buckets. A current marketwide AI-accelerator chip-revenue percentage remains unobservable in these filings. [Nvidia Q3 CFO commentary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/q3fy27cfocommentary.htm) · [AMD 10-Q](https://ir.amd.com/financial-information/sec-filings/content/0000002488-26-000191/amd-20260926.htm) · [Broadcom Q3 release](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-inc-announces-third-quarter-fiscal-year-2026-financial)

**Forecast treatment:** evaluate custom silicon through Nvidia’s realized shipments, pricing and margins. Do not mechanically multiply management’s revenue forecast by 28%, or subtract an additional historical-to-future share loss from it. That would combine incompatible measures. Continued sales growth remains possible; lower pricing power and slower growth after current supply expands are the more useful risks to examine.

## 5. Financing and governance affect the quality of earnings as well as volume

Nvidia’s neocloud arrangements total $32B of contingent commitments. They take effect only after financing, deployment, operation and Nvidia acceptance. Maximum guarantees total $118B, including $105B supporting PORTS-Pike, phased as buildings enter service starting FY2029. These figures are neither additional chip revenue nor certain near-term losses. (7. See [7] Financing, cash conversion, and governance below.)

A financing contraction can remove a buyer before a chip ships. A guarantee can also leave Nvidia exposed after the initial hardware sale. Reallocation to a better-capitalized buyer would preserve some sales, but the replacement buyer may favor its own chips. The combined effect is not disclosed. CoreWeave’s expected customer prepayments provide a counterweight to the claim that all neocloud expansion depends on Nvidia support. (5. See [5] American customer demand and deployment plans below.)

**Cash conversion requires attention.** Q3 operating cash flow was $48.253B against $65.287B of net income. Nvidia reported **$43.777B** of free cash flow after equipment and intangible asset purchases and principal payments. Receivable days increased from 60 to 64, which Nvidia attributes partly to shipment timing and extended terms for investment-grade customers. Inventory growth was concentrated in inputs and work-in-process relative to year-end. [Nvidia’s earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/q3fy27pr.htm) · (Financing and cash-flow evidence. See [7] Financing, cash conversion, and governance below.)

Nvidia reported **$113.672B** of nine-month free cash flow. Subtracting $58.100B of equity purchases and adding $9.493B of corresponding sale proceeds yields **$65.065B** before other uses. That $65.065B is an analytical cash-use measure. The valuation model excludes equity gains and holds diluted shares constant. (Financing and cash-flow evidence. ## 7. Nvidia has a $186 normal earnings target and $118 cash value

## 8. Cash conversion determines long term ownership value

A separate five-year discounted free-cash-flow model tests ownership value as the sector matures. Annual periods end each November to align with the valuation date. The first year includes part of FY2027 before the larger FY2028 ramp. The model assumes competition and capacity growth progressively reduce operating margins to 42%. It deducts net operating reinvestment, including capital expenditure less depreciation and changes in working capital. The separate ecosystem deduction estimates future net economic cash absorbed by partner funding after recovery, rather than expensing every dollar invested. These ratios are assumptions.

| Rolling year ending November | 2027 | 2028 | 2029 | 2030 | 2031 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue, $B | 565 | 665 | 690 | 705 | 720 |
| Operating margin | 59% | 57% | 51% | 46% | 42% |
| Net operating reinvestment / revenue | 7.5% | 8.0% | 7.0% | 6.0% | 5.5% |
| Net ecosystem cash absorption / revenue | 3.0% | 3.0% | 2.5% | 2.0% | 2.0% |
| Modeled free cash flow to firm, $B | 217.4 | 241.5 | 226.5 | 212.8 | 197.0 |

Free cash flow = revenue × [operating margin × (1 − 17% tax) − reinvestment share − ecosystem cash absorption share]. The 10% discount rate and 3.5% perpetual nominal growth are analyst assumptions. At the fifth year-end, ongoing business value equals year-five cash flow × 1.035 ÷ (0.10 − 0.035). Discount all five annual cash flows and the ongoing business to today. Add $21.14B net cash and marketable debt after debt, and $54.87B for a 50% realization on $109.75B of reported marketable equity and non-marketable securities. Divide by 24.186B latest quarterly diluted weighted-average shares. The portfolio haircut reflects volatility and illiquidity. A move from 50% realization to zero or full carrying value changes the result by about $2.27 a share in either direction. [Balance sheet, investment measurement and share basis](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/supplementalfinancialinfor.htm).

| Current $/share by discount rate | 2.5% perpetual growth | 3.5% | 4.5% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9% | 122 | 138 | 161 |
| 10% | 107 | **118** | 134 |
| 11% | 95 | 104 | 115 |

Roughly 70% of operating value comes from cash beyond year five. To justify $125.53 at the same 10% discount rate with the projected five annual cash flows, perpetual nominal growth must reach 4.01%. Alternatively, fixing perpetual growth at 3.5% and holding years one through four unchanged requires $213.9B in year-five free cash flow. At the assumed $720B of sales, that takes a 44.8% operating margin versus the model’s 42%. The less demanding near-term earnings hurdle and the more demanding long-term cash hurdle explain the hold. The close is 6.2% above the central cash value. A 20% discount yields $94.56. A full filing with clean controls, stable or improving collection time and lower reliance on Nvidia-backed financing would justify revisiting that entry price.

A commitment to purchase hardware creates a supplier liability or obligation. A buyer’s reservation for cloud capacity creates a potential future service payment. Between them lie credit risk, site commissioning and collections. Nvidia’s position on both sides of parts of the buildout is why the model allows for partner funding and discounts its investment holdings. [CFO commentary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/q3fy27cfocommentary.htm)

Nvidia generated $113.67B of company-defined free cash flow on $183.30B net income in the first nine months, a 62.0% conversion. Equity purchases of $58.10B less $9.49B of sale proceeds absorbed another $48.61B. That leaves $65.07B, or 22.2% of revenue, after those investments and before acquisitions or financing. Buying securities creates assets of uncertain exit value. Receivables reached $81.03B and collection time rose to 64 days from 60. Management attributes this to the Rubin shipment schedule and longer payment terms for some investment-grade customers. Two customers held 35% of receivables. Inventory rose to $39.72B from $31.6B in the preceding quarter. Watch the combination of receivables, customer concentration and facility acceptance as shipments scale. [Earnings and cash-flow reconciliation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/q3fy27pr.htm) · [Balance sheet and customer concentration](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/supplementalfinancialinfor.htm) · [Payment terms and inventory](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/q3fy27cfocommentary.htm).

### A production fleet gives separate input and output costs

DeepSeek published a day of V3/R1 traffic on February 27–28, 2025. Eight Nvidia H800s per node at its assumed $2 per GPU-hour imply $16 per node-hour. Its separate prompt-reading and token-generation fleets let us price each phase. A million means 1,000,000 tokens throughout. This is a historical production observation from one operator.

| DeepSeek V3/R1 | What it measured | GPU lease equivalent per million |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Input, prompt processing | 73,700 input tokens/second per node, including cache hits | **$0.060 per 1M input** |
| Output, generation | 14,800 output tokens/second per node, averaging 20–22 generated tokens/second per user | **$0.300 per 1M output** |
| Actual daily mix | 608B input and 168B output; 56.3% of input hit the disk cache | **$0.112 per 1M total** |

For each phase, cost = 1,000,000 × $16 ÷ (3,600 × node tokens/second). The separate estimates reproduce $87.1K for the day, within 0.1% of the company's $87,072. ## 14. What changes for Nvidia’s forecast and valuation

**Next several quarters.** Nvidia’s call still describes demand above available supply, and first production Rubin racks are running at several large cloud customers. AWS’s unit plan and Anthropic’s explicitly Nvidia-powered contracts support continuing demand. A site delay, export restriction or financing failure can still push Nvidia shipments out even when end-user demand exists. Capacity can be reallocated when another buyer has compatible power, site readiness, funding, export eligibility and delivery timing. That distinction matters because the $390B supply commitment is largely procurement of inputs and Nvidia must manage inventory when product eligibility or demand changes. [Nvidia call](https://s201.q4cdn.com/141608511/files/content_files/NVDA-3Q27-Earnings-Transcript.pdf) · [Anthropic contracts](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm)

**Beyond the guidance year.** Customers can grow total spending while shifting more of the next workload to custom silicon. A lower cost per successful task can drive usage up. If realized revenue per token halves, paid token volume must more than double for token revenue to grow, and required chip capacity also depends on each generation’s throughput. Model vendor gross margins give some evidence that paying customers value the service. They do not establish that every accelerator deployment can earn an adequate return after R&D, credit costs and utilization risk. For Nvidia valuation, assess whether booked revenue converts to cash and whether Nvidia retains pricing power as the buyers above gain credible alternatives.

Policy, custom-chip penetration, and financing can affect the valuation through different lines. A project delay primarily changes shipment timing and working capital. A switch to a competitive accelerator can change volumes, mix and gross margin. An underperforming investment, guaranteed buyer or backstopped project can reduce realized cash and raise the multiple risk even if system sales initially close. There is no public reconciliation to a reliable FY2029–30 U.S. GPU order book. The reverse valuation therefore remains a conditional calculation.

## Evidence

Remote-access rule: specified advanced-computing items and restricted-country end-use/ownership; aggregate TPP thresholds; licensing and recommender-system provisions. Existing affected providers have a transition provision. It is not a blanket ban on ordinary American AI workloads.

New York pauses specified discretionary environmental permits for up to a year. Texas confirms a pause on permits directly supporting data-center infrastructure. Neither establishes that every existing site stops operating.

November 20: President states opposition to American companies building overseas data centers. The reviewed release does not provide an implementing prohibition, tax rate or effective date; treat as policy risk after Nvidia’s November 17 call.

## Analytical transmission

Restrictions can reduce eligible demand; permit/grid delays reduce capacity ready to accept chips. Reallocation works only if another lawful buyer has financing and a suitable site. With baseline demand D, deliverable supply S, and net demand lost L, supply remains binding only if D − L > S. The public record does not quantify all three.

## Formula and share basis

EPS = (revenue × gross margin − operating expense) × (1 − tax rate) / 24.186. Other income is zero; stock compensation remains within modeled operating expense. Shares are held at the Q3 diluted weighted average, not a forecast of outstanding shares. No added investment-asset value, buyback benefit, separate guarantee charge or probability weighting. Value = EPS × selected forward P/E. The model therefore does not capture every possible liability, restatement or financing outcome.
